# Agreement and disagreement

Agreement and disagreement are derived from structured claims and eligible evidence, not source count or editorial symmetry.

## Independent support

Support is counted by underlying work and source-family relationships. Exact duplicates, article versions, attributed republications, and syndicated copies do not create independent corroboration. A primary source and an independently reporting secondary source may count separately. Related listening, transcript-free podcast metadata, and background that does not state the proposition count zero.

Agreement strength ranges from single source through limited or multi-source corroboration to primary-plus-independent and broad corroboration. Agreement is not proof, and meaningful differences in timing, units, scope, qualification, or attribution remain visible.

## Disagreement

Disagreements may be factual, quantitative, temporal, scope-based, causal, interpretive, forecast-based, attribution-based, apparent only, or resolved by a source update. Each side preserves its proposition, support and independence counts, primary-source role, supersession state, and evidence strength.

Different time periods, actual versus forecast, revenue versus margin, background context, compatible wording, and corrected prior versions must not become manufactured conflict. Conversely, primary evidence is not flattened into equal weight with a weaker interpretation. The product represents real uncertainty without forcing false balance.

`pnpm discourse:validate` reports false-consensus, false-disagreement, syndication, supersession, and evidence-strength failures individually.
