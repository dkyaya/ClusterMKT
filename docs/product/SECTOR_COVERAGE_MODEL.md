# Sector coverage model

Sector following is a distinct editorial product, not an alias for following the largest constituents. A sector follower receives a shared edition brief plus accepted Story Clusters classified as sector-wide, company-led sector impact, macro-to-sector, or high-materiality company-specific.

## Scope labels

- **Sector-wide** requires documented breadth: multiple materially affected constituents or subindustries, a sector-level primary action, or cross-company supply, demand, pricing, capacity, or regulatory evidence.
- **Company-led sector impact** begins with one issuer but retains that label when evidence establishes wider propagation.
- **Macro-to-sector** requires a specific transmission path from macroeconomic or policy evidence into the sector. A generic macro mention does not qualify.
- **Company-specific** remains the default for an issuer event without sector propagation. It enters a sector feed only when its demonstrated sector relevance crosses the stricter inclusion threshold.

Market capitalization alone is never propagation evidence. Headline-only metadata, incidental names or ticker tokens, syndicated copies of one report, and podcast metadata without a reviewed transcript cannot promote a story.

## Shared Sector Brief

One deterministic brief is assembled for each sector, market date, and edition. It references accepted active Story Clusters rather than raw articles and can be reused for every follower of that sector. Personalization selects and ranks shared outputs; it does not regenerate the brief.

The brief reports development and scope counts, key themes, affected subindustries, agreement, competing arguments, uncertainty, coverage gaps, and evidence that could change the picture. It does not claim completeness, synthesize unavailable content, make recommendations, or predict prices.

## Ranking and breadth

Material sector-wide developments rank first, followed by company-led impact, macro-to-sector, qualifying company-specific events, and context. When at least three issuers qualify, one issuer receives no more than two company-led or company-specific positions in the first ten and cannot exceed 30 percent of that window. Sector-wide and macro clusters are exempt. When scores are close, uncovered subindustries receive a transparent breadth preference; clearly stronger material news is not suppressed merely to create variety.

Uncovered subindustries are reported as gaps. The system never fabricates a lower-ranked company or generic sector card to fill a gap.

## Current boundary

Everything in this phase is offline, fixture-driven, deterministic, and labeled as demonstration data. Live ingestion, publisher connectivity, databases, authentication, AI inference, TTS, price data, and private-market coverage remain unimplemented.

Upstream sector evidence now has a deterministic normalization contract: syndicated copies count once, accepted entities are distinct from candidates, and event signatures prevent unrelated issuer events from merging merely because company and date match. Sector evaluation remains unchanged and consumes no live sources.
